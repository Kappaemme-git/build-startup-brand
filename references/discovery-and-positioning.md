# Discovery and positioning

Use this reference to turn incomplete startup context into a defensible strategic core.

## Discovery order

Inspect supplied evidence before asking questions. Recover as much as possible from:

- product copy, pitch, repository, screenshots, onboarding, or demo
- pricing, calls to action, customer language, testimonials, and product behavior
- existing logo, colors, typography, content, and sales materials
- named competitors, current workarounds, and the cost of doing nothing

Ask only about material gaps. A useful compact question set is:

1. What does the product help a specific customer accomplish?
2. Who feels the problem most urgently, and what triggers them to look for help?
3. What do they use or do today instead?
4. What makes this product credibly better or different?
5. What should the brand feel like, and what must it never resemble?

Replace questions with narrower ones when some answers are already known.

## Evidence labels

Keep these categories distinct:

- **Fact** — Visible in a supplied artifact or reliable source.
- **Founder claim** — Stated by the user but not independently demonstrated.
- **Inference** — A reasoned interpretation from available context.
- **Recommendation** — A deliberate strategic or creative choice.
- **Unknown** — Important information that still needs validation.

Do not upgrade a claim or inference into a fact through repetition.

## Strategic core

Produce each component in one or two precise sentences.

### Primary audience

Name the smallest commercially meaningful group with a shared situation, not a demographic cloud. Include the role, context, trigger, and desired progress.

Weak: “small businesses.”

Stronger: “Owner-operated Italian fitness studios opening a second location and losing track of member follow-up across WhatsApp and spreadsheets.”

### Competitive frame

State what the audience compares the startup with at decision time. Include:

- direct products
- indirect tools
- manual workarounds
- doing nothing

### Distinctive value

Explain the mechanism that creates a preferable outcome. “Better,” “simpler,” and “AI-powered” are not mechanisms.

### Proof

Use only available proof:

- demonstrated product capability
- founder expertise
- customer behavior or outcomes
- technical advantage
- operational constraint competitors cannot easily copy

When proof is missing, convert the claim into a hypothesis and add a validation task.

### Positioning statement

Use this internal structure, then rewrite it as natural prose:

> For [primary audience] in [triggering situation], [startup] is the [competitive frame] that [distinctive outcome] because [credible mechanism/proof], unlike [current alternative].

Do not publish the fill-in-the-blank wording as customer-facing copy.

### Salient brand idea

Compress the positioning into one memorable organizing idea. It must:

- connect to the product truth
- matter to the audience
- guide both language and visuals
- exclude plausible competitor positions
- remain useful beyond one campaign

Test: if a competitor could claim it unchanged, sharpen it.

### Brand promise

State the customer outcome the company commits to enabling. Keep it:

- meaningful to the audience
- specific enough to judge
- credible from available proof
- broad enough to support product growth

## Rebrand diagnosis

For `rebrand`, identify before changing anything:

- recognition worth preserving
- current inconsistency
- wrong or outdated perception
- strategic change requiring the rebrand
- migration risks across product and channels

Do not replace recognizable assets merely because they look dated. Change them only when they obstruct the new positioning, fail basic use cases, or create costly inconsistency.

## Research boundaries

When researching:

- prefer primary company sites, product documentation, app listings, and direct customer language
- record URLs and dates for claims that may change
- distinguish visual inspiration from competitor imitation
- never claim trademark clearance from a web search
- avoid collecting sensitive personal information
