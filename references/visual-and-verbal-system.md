# Visual and verbal system

Use this reference after a territory has been selected.

## Verbal identity

### Messaging hierarchy

Create:

- one-line product explanation
- primary tagline
- short value proposition
- three message pillars
- one proof point or validation need for each pillar
- primary call to action

Make the one-line explanation clearer than the tagline. The tagline may create memory; the explanation must create understanding.

### Voice

Define three voice traits as useful tensions:

- direct, not abrupt
- expert, not academic
- energetic, not chaotic

For each trait, provide:

- what it means
- one “do” example
- one “don't” example

Also define:

- preferred vocabulary
- avoided vocabulary
- sentence rhythm
- level of technical detail
- capitalization and punctuation behavior when distinctive

Do not reduce voice to adjectives without examples.

## Visual identity

### Symbol

Build the symbol from the salient idea or product mechanism. Require:

- a one-sentence description
- a simple underlying geometry
- a reason connected to the strategy
- monochrome behavior
- small-size behavior
- a negative-space or silhouette test when relevant

Reject:

- generic sparkles, rockets, globes, shields, brains, infinity loops, or abstract swooshes without a specific mechanism
- visual puns unrelated to the product
- complex marks that need an explanation to be recognized
- obvious imitation of named competitors

Use image generation for exploration, mood, texture, scenes, and art direction. Generated text and logos are concept material until deliberately reconstructed and checked.

### Color

Assign roles, not just hex codes:

- primary brand
- secondary
- accent or action
- background
- text
- border or muted surface

Check text/background contrast. Use WCAG-oriented thresholds as a practical minimum:

- 4.5:1 for normal body text
- 3:1 for large text and essential graphical elements

Flag combinations intended only for decoration. Never imply that an automated contrast check alone proves full accessibility.

### Typography

Specify:

- display family or category
- body family or category
- fallbacks
- weights
- size and spacing behavior
- where each role is used

Prefer fonts with usable licenses and adequate language coverage. Verify current licensing before recommending commercial use.

### Image direction

Define subject, framing, light, texture, motion, color treatment, and forbidden clichés. Tie the direction to the territory rather than producing a generic moodboard.

### Layout behavior

Define:

- grid and spacing character
- corner and border behavior
- density
- hierarchy
- repetition or signature motif
- motion principle when relevant

## Applications

Choose at least four high-value contexts from:

- landing-page hero
- product onboarding
- app icon or favicon
- social launch post
- pitch-deck cover
- sales one-pager
- email header
- packaging or physical touchpoint

For each application, show which strategic message leads and how the visual system behaves. Avoid decorative mockups that do not demonstrate a real decision.

## Final checks

- Does the landing hero explain the product without relying on the tagline?
- Does the identity work without the logo occupying most of the composition?
- Can the system produce ten pieces of content without becoming repetitive?
- Does the symbol work in one color?
- Are text combinations readable?
- Are claims supported?
- Could a named competitor plausibly use the same system unchanged?
- Which parts require trademark, font-license, or specialist design review?
