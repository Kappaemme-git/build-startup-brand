# Creative territories

Use this reference to generate three coherent routes and select between them without relying on taste alone.

## Territory anatomy

Every territory must include:

1. **Name** — A memorable internal label, not necessarily public copy.
2. **Thesis** — The strategic idea expressed by the route.
3. **Audience response** — What the audience should understand and feel.
4. **Metaphor** — The organizing image or behavior.
5. **Tagline direction** — One strong line plus up to two alternates.
6. **Voice** — Three traits and one example sentence.
7. **Symbol direction** — A describable, ownable visual mechanism.
8. **Palette** — Three to five functional colors with roles.
9. **Typography** — Display and body direction with rationale.
10. **Application** — One concrete scene showing the route in use.
11. **Risk** — The most likely misunderstanding or execution failure.

Do not create three cosmetic variants of the same route. Change the organizing metaphor, emotional register, and expression while preserving the strategic core.

## Useful territory contrasts

Choose contrasts appropriate to the startup instead of applying these mechanically:

- precise system vs expressive movement
- trusted guide vs provocative challenger
- quiet confidence vs visible momentum
- human craft vs technical instrument
- protective infrastructure vs liberating tool
- category authority vs category reframing

## Weighted scoring

Score each criterion from 1 to 10, then calculate the weighted result.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic fit | 20% | Does it express the positioning and promise? |
| Audience relevance | 20% | Will the primary audience care and understand? |
| Distinctiveness | 20% | Is it meaningfully different in the competitive frame? |
| Credibility | 15% | Can the product and available proof support it? |
| Memorability | 10% | Can someone recall and describe it later? |
| Extensibility | 10% | Can it scale across product, content, and company growth? |
| Execution resilience | 5% | Does it survive small, monochrome, and low-budget use? |

Calculate:

```text
weighted score =
strategic_fit × 0.20 +
audience_relevance × 0.20 +
distinctiveness × 0.20 +
credibility × 0.15 +
memorability × 0.10 +
extensibility × 0.10 +
execution_resilience × 0.05
```

## Selection rules

- Recommend the highest-scoring route only when no material strategic risk overrides the score.
- Explain what the winner gains and what it gives up.
- Keep disagreement visible when two routes are close.
- Reject a route despite a good score if it resembles a named competitor, depends on an unsupported claim, or fails primary use cases.
- Do not average incompatible routes into a vague hybrid.

## Hybrid rule

Allow a hybrid only when it can be stated like:

> Use Territory A's strategic thesis and voice with Territory C's color behavior and symbol mechanism because both express [same organizing idea].

Reject “take the best bits from each” without a coherent organizing idea.

## Recognition tests

Add these to the validation plan:

- Five-second test: what does the viewer think the startup does?
- One-week recall: what element can the viewer describe from memory?
- Competitor shuffle: can the identity be matched to the correct startup without the name?
- Monochrome test: does the symbol retain its logic without color?
- Favicon test: does the mark remain recognizable at 16–32 pixels?
- Message test: can a target user restate the promise accurately?
